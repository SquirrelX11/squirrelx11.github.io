Verden

Be ready before you lose access.

An encrypted vault for the things that get you back in — recovery phrases, backup codes, keys, and the plan for the day the phone is gone.

iPhoneiPad
Coming soon on the App Store

Features

The day you lose access is the wrong day to start.

A readiness score you can check

One reading for how recoverable you are, and every point in it traced back to something you entered. It never guesses and never inspects your accounts.

The map of what depends on what

One mailbox behind eleven accounts is a single point of failure you cannot see in a list. The map draws it, and names what falls over with it.

Drills before the day itself

Lost phone, stolen laptop, closed mailbox. Run the scenario and Verden tells you what you would still get back into — and what you would not.

Passwords, keys and phrases

Seed phrases, private keys, API secrets and backup codes, encrypted with everything else and concealed until you ask. Optional autofill for the passwords.

A capsule you can hand over

Seal what someone would need into one file with its own key. Nothing readable sits outside the seal, and what goes in is decided per capsule.

Your iCloud, or nothing at all

Sync is optional and end-to-end encrypted with a key that never leaves your device. Apple stores ciphertext. So would we, if we stored anything.

Honest about the limits

What it does, and what it will not pretend.

Nobody can open it for you

Your Master Recovery Phrase is generated on the device, shown once and stored nowhere — not with us, not in iCloud. There is no escrow, no reset and no support procedure that can stand in for it. Write it down.

It does not inspect your accounts

Verden cannot read Apple Passwords, list your passkeys or ask a service whether your two-factor is on. Everything it shows is built from what you entered or confirmed — and it says so on the screen where it shows it.
